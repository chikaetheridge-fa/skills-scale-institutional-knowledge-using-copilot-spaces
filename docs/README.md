# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a structured, iterative approach to project management that prioritizes customer value, clear ownership, and data-informed decisions. Our process spans from project initiation through delivery, release, and continuous improvement.

OctoAcme's project management approach is structured around a clear lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective. At the start, teams validate a project idea through a one-pager that defines the problem, goal, success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, risks, and resource needs. Once approved, the team moves into planning by creating a prioritized backlog, estimating scope, establishing the Definition of Done, identifying dependencies, and setting milestones and release expectations. This ensures work is translated from a business need into an actionable plan with accountable owners and measurable outcomes.

The process emphasizes defined roles and shared accountability. Product leaders define outcomes and prioritize the roadmap, project managers coordinate schedules, dependencies, communications, and risk management, and developers are responsible for building, testing, and delivering features that meet acceptance criteria. QA/testing helps validate quality and user acceptance, while stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context. This blend of personas keeps work aligned across product, engineering, and leadership while supporting a customer-first, iterative delivery model.

Communication is intentionally regular and structured. The organization recommends weekly PM/PdM alignment, delivery team standups, milestone-based stakeholder updates, and ad hoc escalation when blockers emerge. Project artifacts such as the charter, risk register, roadmap, and status reports serve as a single source of truth, while escalation pathways move from the team to the PM, then Product Lead, and ultimately sponsor-level support for business-impacting issues. Risk management is also built into the rhythm: teams identify, assess, mitigate, and monitor risks at regular syncs, with clear owners and contingency plans for dependencies and cross-team impacts.

Quality assurance is treated as a continuous practice rather than a final step. The documentation recommends unit, integration, and end-to-end testing as appropriate; CI checks for tests, linting, and security scans; and manual QA for acceptance validation when needed. Release readiness requires passing CI, meeting acceptance criteria, documenting rollback plans, and running smoke tests before production deployment. After delivery, teams conduct retrospectives to capture lessons learned, assign action items, and improve the process over time. This creates a repeatable system that balances speed, accountability, and reliability.

## Core Principles

- **Customer-first**: Prioritize customer value and usability
- **Iterative delivery**: Deliver small, testable increments
- **Clear ownership**: Each project has named ownership (PM & Product Lead)
- **Data-informed**: Measure impact and iterate based on evidence
- **Psychological safety**: Encourage feedback and learning

## Project Lifecycle

1. **Initiation**: Define problem, stakeholders, and high-level timeline
2. **Planning**: Break into shippable increments, identify risks and dependencies
3. **Execution**: Build, test, review, and iterate
4. **Release**: Deploy, verify, and announce
5. **Retrospective**: Capture learnings and continuous improvements
